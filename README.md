# Bubble and particle collision

[![checks](https://github.com/hashimishaque-platform/bubble-particle-collision/actions/workflows/checks.yml/badge.svg)](https://github.com/hashimishaque-platform/bubble-particle-collision/actions/workflows/checks.yml)

The flow around a rising bubble, the particles that hit it, and synthetic PIV of the same flow,
for flotation. The program solves the Navier-Stokes equations around the bubble, follows particles
with the Maxey-Riley equation and computes the collision efficiency. It is a single-page browser
program: everything is computed in the browser, with no server and no libraries.

**Open the program:** https://hashimishaque.com/bubble-particle-collision/

![Flow around a 1 mm bubble with frother and the paths of 30 µm quartz particles](screenshot.png)

## About the data

This is a theoretical simulation. The flow is computed from the Navier-Stokes equations, the
particles and the PIV images are generated by the program, and the comparison values come from
published equations and correlations. No measured data, and no data from any research centre,
were used.

## What it does

- Solves the steady, axisymmetric Navier-Stokes equations for water flowing past a spherical
  bubble, for an immobile surface (water with frother) or a mobile one (clean water). The rise
  speed comes from a force balance with the Schiller-Naumann or the Mei, Klausner and Lawrence
  drag correlation.
- Follows particles through the flow with the Maxey-Riley equation (drag, settling, pressure
  gradient and added mass) and finds the collision efficiency from interception, settling and
  inertia, next to the Sutherland, Weber-Paddock and Yoon-Luttrell correlations.
- Renders two synthetic PIV frames of tracers that move with the computed flow and evaluates them
  with FFT cross-correlation, a sub-pixel peak fit, a second pass with window offset and the
  normalised median test. Because the exact flow is known, every PIV vector can be checked, which
  shows where PIV next to a bubble surface stops being reliable.
- Draws a cut through a bubble swarm in a flotation cell: bubbles that rise at the speed given by
  the Tomiyama drag correlation, the potential flow each one sets up in the liquid, and synthetic
  turbulence (kinematic simulation with the Kolmogorov spectrum) for a chosen energy dissipation
  rate. The bubbles can be a random swarm or a chain above one needle, with a chosen surface
  tension, and a Play button lets them rise. A file of bubble tracks can be loaded instead of the
  generated swarm; it is read in the browser only and is not part of the program. The page then
  plays its time steps and compares each track's size, rise speed and shape with the correlations.

- Runs a live two-dimensional flow simulation (lattice Boltzmann) of liquid streaming past a bubble
  held in place, with an optional grid of rods upstream that makes the oncoming flow turbulent.
  It shows the shed eddies, averages the turbulent kinetic energy and dissipation rate around the
  bubble, and counts where particles hit it, on the front or in the turbulent wake at the rear.

## Using it for research

- **Data out.** Every figure has a download button: CSV for curves and fields, JSON for the
  settings and results. Each file records the program version and the settings used.
- **Shareable cases.** "Link to these settings" puts every input into the web address, so a
  link reproduces the case. Sections have their own addresses: `#piv`, `#swarm`, `#live-flow`,
  `#model`, `#validity`.
- **Batch runs.** `node cli.js case.json --csv result.csv` runs the same code without a browser;
  see [docs/cli.md](docs/cli.md) and the case files in `examples/`.
- **Range of validity.** The page lists the range of each model and what was checked; the
  numerical methods and grid-convergence data are in [docs/numerics.md](docs/numerics.md).
- **Tutorials.** [Collision efficiency of quartz](docs/tutorials/01-collision-efficiency.md),
  [turbulence behind a bubble](docs/tutorials/02-turbulence-behind-a-bubble.md),
  [rise speed, shape and tracks files](docs/tutorials/03-rise-speed-and-tracks.md).
- **Checks on every change.** The workflow in `.github/workflows/checks.yml` runs `checks.js`
  and the command-line examples, rebuilds the page and fails if the committed results are not
  reproduced.

## Method in short

| Part | Method |
|---|---|
| Flow | Stream function and vorticity on a grid uniform in ln r and θ. Second-order central differences, with deferred correction for the convection terms. SOR for the stream function, under-relaxation for the vorticity. |
| Drag | From the surface vorticity and its normal derivative. |
| Particles | Maxey-Riley equation without the history and lift forces, added-mass coefficient 1/2. Fourth-order Runge-Kutta, or an exponential integrator below a Stokes number of 0.02, where the equation is stiff. |
| Collision efficiency | Exact from the particle stream function for particles without inertia; bisection on trajectories that start 8 bubble radii upstream for particles with inertia. |
| Bubble swarm | Rise speed from Tomiyama et al. (1998), shape from Wellek et al. (1966). Each bubble is a potential-flow dipole of its volume-equivalent sphere; the fields are added. Turbulence: 200 random Fourier modes, frozen in time (Fung et al. 1992). |
| Live flow simulation | D2Q9 lattice Boltzmann, two relaxation times, Smagorinsky subgrid model, Zou-He inlet, damping layer before the outlet; 20 cells per bubble diameter. |
| Synthetic PIV | Gaussian particle images integrated over the pixels, with background, noise and 8-bit quantisation. 32 px windows at 50 % overlap by default. |

## Limitations

- Steady, axisymmetric flow past a spherical bubble, so the bubble diameter is limited to 1.2 mm
  with frother (Re below about 160) and 0.7 mm in clean water.
- The surface is either fully immobile or fully mobile.
- Collision only, not attachment: no film drainage, no surface forces. The particle does not
  disturb the flow.
- The swarm figure is kinematic: potential flow around each bubble (no wakes), fields simply
  added, and frozen synthetic turbulence that does not interact with the bubbles.
- The live simulation is two-dimensional (an elliptic cylinder, not a bubble), with an immobile
  surface and a coarse grid; it shows mechanisms, not numbers for a real cell.
- The PIV images are idealised: motion in the light sheet only, no reflections at the bubble.
